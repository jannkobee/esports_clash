# Lightweight living world — 9 November 2026

## Owner decision and scope

The owner authorized integrating the living-world engine while protecting the current machine. The latest instruction supersedes **global** AI shutdown in the attached takeover specification: club management retires as humans take over, but tournament organizers, streamers, analysts and player Wire identities remain able to react to actual public events. This uses ordinary backend logic and the existing finite social grammar; it requires no model, provider credits, GPU, local LLM or separate process per character.

This delivery connects and bounds existing management/media systems. It does **not** establish completion of the entire attached career, consent/contract negotiation, sponsorship, delegation or multi-year economic acceptance specification. Existing payroll, markets, staff appointments, development, competitions and social memories continue through their canonical services. No competing economy or match engine was added.

## Coordinator and resource policy

- `LivingWorldEngine`, `AdvanceLivingWorld`, `config/living_world.php`, migration 000016 and `routes/console.php` replace the 15-minute per-bot job fan-out with one coalesced `world` queue batch every five minutes. At the inspected 131-bot population, the old manager schedule alone could dispatch 12,576 jobs/day; the coordinator admits at most 288 scheduled batches/day. These are admission counts, **not a measured CPU saving**.
- The same default worker consumes `default,world` in priority order. No additional worker/container is introduced. The existing rankings worker also uses persistent PHP with a ten-second idle poll, 256-MB process memory threshold and 25-job/one-hour recycling; its connection, queue, timeout, retries and ranking logic are unchanged. Restart either persistent worker after relevant PHP source changes. `queue:work` replaces the development default worker's framework-reloading `queue:listen`, with a 192-MB process memory threshold, 250-job/one-hour recycling and existing 384-MB/0.5-CPU container limits. Restart this persistent worker after application changes; it no longer automatically reloads each job.
- Each batch selects at most eight indexed due clubs, with an eight-second soft wall-clock budget. Management gets half the soft budget so social work has room. Atomic domain actions finish safely and may cross the soft deadline; the queue job has a 25-second timeout. This is cooperative scheduling, not hard real-time preemption. Existing transactional rollback applies on a terminated connection.
- Reviews ordinarily become due in 120–136 minutes. A durable 64-event cursor can advance affected clubs to a 15-minute review; already-considered events, including a manager's own committed actions, cannot create a feedback loop. Old historical events are not replayed at migration. Never-reviewed clubs and oldest due work get a fair turn. A failed review is logged, counted and deferred for 30 minutes while other clubs may proceed.
- Market consideration examines at most 12 closing auctions and six not-yet-entered competitions per review. Preferences, legal compatibility, wage reserves, escrow, fees and registration rules still decide actual actions. This intentionally trades exhaustive/immediate response for bounded work.
- Optional work defers while any online match is allocated/running, 20 ready core jobs wait, or a ready core job has waited over 120 seconds. Reserved, delayed and `world` jobs do not inflate that pressure signal. Core scheduling, human actions, training, payroll, contract expiry, official competitions/Nations Cup, match execution/settlement and frozen ML/Colab/visual pipelines remain independent.
- Unique dispatch plus a transactionally locked durable pending-queue check prevents batches accumulating behind core work even after cache expiry/restart. Old per-club and voice/recovery jobs drain into the coordinator. The hourly payroll pass no longer duplicates bot recruitment.
- Media recovery runs on source changes or at most every 15 minutes while idle. Registry admission/refresh runs hourly and is bounded/resumable. Without a new source, social consideration backs off to 30 minutes. Existing voice caps remain three posts/12 decisions per invocation, 18 posts/hour, 144/day, eight-hour author cooldown and four reactions/source. Source changes do not bypass these caps. Successful-post quotas and silence are intentional; a batch need not publish anything.
- Known factual posts avoid repeated registry writes; daily spotlights skip already-published aggregations, and completed-match recovery excludes already-covered results. Development consideration is capped at 20 observations. Long scans/loops honor the shared deadline. Factual live-broadcast notices and accepted-result dispatch remain independent of optional opinions.

## Human authority and lifecycle

Migration 000016 preserves existing records and adds `management_epoch`, indexed due times and the singleton durable runtime. Existing populated worlds start `active`; an empty founding world starts `awaiting_population`. A final completed takeover persists `retired` for **management only**. Social processing still runs in that state. There is no automatic reactivation.

Takeover still uses the canonical catalog/user/club transaction, retains the same club ID, players, funds, staff, commitments and history, advances its ownership epoch and clears optional review admission. No replacement population job is dispatched. Market/roster actions hold canonical locks; later tournament registration rechecks bot status and epoch under the event/club transaction. Automatic club invitation acceptance also rechecks authority at commit. Pending speculative decisions lose authority; binding auction bids and accepted tournament/fixture commitments are retained.

Old serialized population jobs lack explicit owner admission and now return without creating clubs. Founding/manual population requests carry an owner-request flag and are rejected after retirement; direct bot provisioning checks retirement inside the catalog lock. A human club is never automatically converted back to a bot.

The prior weak Tier-5 newcomer rule remains intact. The broader specification's policy for eventual higher-tier human takeovers remains unresolved; no high-tier roster advantage is silently granted to a new account. There is no new takeover UI for existing human managers in this delivery.

## Shared game and backend

`WorldSimulation`, the game `WorldDesk` and the owner backend World Desk expose the same management lifecycle, lightweight mode, review horizon and pause reason. Owner operations include last batch duration, reviews, failures and due-club count. Public read projections exclude internal wallets, bids and staff orders. `LIVING_WORLD_ENABLED=false` is an explicit operational pause for optional coordination; it does not stop core gameplay. `WORLD_VOICES_ENABLED` independently controls social opinions. Neither setting calls a cloud model.
