# Clash Arena: team play, card identity and individual avatar mastery

Starting lessons for a learned command controller. Strategic advice can be revised by experience; mechanical rules remain enforced.

## Actual game rules

- Destroy the opposing outer turret, inner turret and Nexus turret in that order. Deeper structures are protected until their preceding turrets are destroyed.
- After the Nexus turret falls, destroy all three barracks: melee, ranged and catapult. The Nexus remains protected until all three are destroyed.
- Destroy the exposed opposing Nexus to win. Kills, damage, gold and a long match are not substitutes for winning.
- Attacks and spells obey their actual range, mana, cooldown, level, targeting and crowd-control rules. Terrain stays solid. Avatars use soft spacing.
- Use information available to your team. Do not read invisible enemy positions, hidden camp health or private opponent economy.

## Player card and avatar identity

- The player card is the competitor; the avatar is the kit they control. Learn the combination rather than confusing their identities.
- Observe the card's stable ID, fictional display alias, six attributes, preferred-role fit, signatures, morale, fatigue and both personalities. These inform decisions; they do not grant extra damage, range or immunity.
- IQ describes game sense, LAN mechanics, TF teamfight execution, CLU pressure play, STA resilience and FLX adaptability. High attributes do not guarantee a correct decision.
- A bodyguard personality can learn protective opportunities and an aggressive personality can learn timing. Neither is a mandatory script or permission to ignore a losing outcome.
- The shared policy learns across card/avatar combinations. Do not claim a particular card mastered an avatar unless that pairing has enough independent evaluated evidence.

## Advance through the correct objectives

- Identify the next attackable structure rather than wasting attacks on a protected building. You may move anywhere legal, but protection prevents damage.
- Pressure structures when enemies cannot punish you effectively. A nearby enemy, an incoming wave, health and available allies change the value of pushing.
- A destroyed barracks empowers its corresponding allied minion class. Once all three fall and the Nexus opens, turn a safe advantage into a finish.

## Farm and use minion waves

- Secure valuable last hits when the target is within reach and your hit can finish it. Use farming to gain levels and resources for later fights.
- Avoid abandoning every wave to chase an enemy. Compare a reliable last hit or structure hit with the time and risk of a chase.
- Carry roles usually need more farm; supports can spend more time helping allies. These are starting priorities, not restrictions on who may farm.
- A friendly wave can absorb structure fire and help a siege. A wave is an opportunity, not a mandatory permission slip for every attack.

## Choose useful fights and targets

- Compare health, nearby team numbers, distance and the damage you can actually deliver. An accessible low-health enemy may be worth finishing.
- Do not attack a target merely because it exists. Consider whether a minion, structure, objective or regrouping move offers more value.
- Coordinate pressure on reachable opponents. Trading a death can be worthwhile if it helps win the game; deaths are measured rather than universally forbidden.

## Manage health, mana and cooldowns

- Use damage spells when they have a useful target or useful area effect. Avoid spending mana on an empty fight just because a cooldown is ready.
- A costly spell is less attractive when mana is scarce. A finishing hit, protection for an ally or a decisive fight can justify the cost.
- Compare continued fighting with moving toward a healing relic, the fountain or a recall. Low health is evidence to consider, not an automatic retreat command.

## Move with a purpose and avoid incoming damage

- Choose destinations that improve your next action: attack range, an objective, an ally, recovery or a safer firing position.
- Notice visible incoming projectiles and their projected paths. Moving away from an imminent impact can be more valuable than another attack.
- Ranged avatars can use reach; melee avatars need a practical approach. Terrain and crowds affect routes. Walking and attack orders can be changed as the situation develops.

## Support allies and protect damage dealers

- Move toward allies when joining them creates a useful local advantage. Nearby allies and enemies matter more than assuming the whole team is present.
- Use targeted shields and protection on an appropriate ally, including yourself when the spell permits it. A damaged ally under pressure may benefit more than a healthy one.
- Tanks and fighters can create space; assassins can seek accessible threats; mages and marksmen can deliver damage; supports can protect and enable. Roles are context for learning, not fixed scripts.

## Understand turret and Nexus danger

- Enemy defensive structures deal real damage and can punish you while you attack. Consider exposure, health, allies and what the attack could gain.
- Tower dives are permitted strategic choices. Experience should determine when the expected gain is worth the risk.
- Moving away from danger can be a good choice. There is no compulsory dive abort, forced sprint or strategic tower-risk veto in this controller.

## Use camps and contest major objectives

- Ordinary camps provide resources but cost time and health. Compare them with a nearby wave, a structure opportunity or helping the team.
- Embermaw and Gravemarch are major objectives. Consider the boss's visible health, your resources and nearby allies and opponents before committing.
- During a contest, decide between fighting opponents, protecting allies, disengaging and finishing the boss. There is no fixed boss-health threshold forcing that decision.

## Use available information and wards

- Known map locations can guide movement without revealing whether a hidden camp is alive or how much health it has.
- Place a ward when information could help a fight, approach or objective contest. The current interface places it at your location, so movement helps determine placement.
- Reconsider a target that disappears. Moving to its last known location is different from tracking its hidden current position.

## Recover and complete useful recalls

- Recall channels for 2.5 seconds and can return you to the healing fountain. Compare this with walking, taking a relic or staying for a valuable action.
- Once a useful recall has started, allow it to finish when circumstances remain favorable. Movement can cancel it when the situation changes.
- Damage and control effects still interrupt according to game mechanics. Recovery is valuable because it enables later play, not because staying in base is a goal.

## Use individual abilities and Kaelen's spell system

- Choose among legal skill, ultimate and target commands. Healing, shielding, control, damage and movement effects are defined by each avatar's actual kit.
- For Kaelen, choose ice, wind or fire orbs, invoke a three-orb recipe, and choose an available invoked spell. Orb sequence and invoked spell identity are distinct model inputs.
- Explore different sequences and timing. The former authored combo planner does not choose the sequence for this learned controller.

## Turn advantages into a win and learn from results

- After a favorable exchange, compare pushing, taking a major objective, farming and recovering. Avoid treating kills as the end of the task.
- Protect your own progress while creating pressure on the enemy base. Which trade is best depends on the state and what the team can accomplish next.
- Learning updates may become weaker or stronger. Use independent complete matches, both sides and uncertainty to judge improvement; training loss and changed choices alone do not establish it.

## Tank

- Create useful space, interrupt threats and make allied damage possible. Compare engaging with protecting an ally.
- Durability is a resource. Taking damage, standing in front or dying first is not success by itself.
- A catcher, an area initiator, a mobile disruptor and a sustained brawler require different spacing and timing.

## Fighter

- Compare short trades, sustained contact and protecting a nearby ally using your actual shield, movement and control tools.
- Enter with a practical follow-up or exit plan; after a useful exchange consider the wave and next structure.
- Your kit may favor ranged poke, summons, displacement or territory rather than permanent melee pursuit.

## Assassin

- Look for an accessible valuable threat and account for protection, control, travel time and the position after a combo.
- Compare spending burst now with waiting for a better opening; abandoning a failed chase can be correct.
- A takedown helps when it improves the team's chance to finish the game. Kill stealing is not a separate objective.

## Mage

- Use actual cast ranges, areas, costs and control to deliver pressure while keeping a useful firing position.
- Compare wave clear, poke, interruption, allied protection and burst; mana and cooldown availability change the trade.
- Channel mages, close burst mages, mobile mages and spellbook mages need different preparation.

## Marksman

- Maintain useful attack uptime, use reach and movement between attacks, and choose a reachable target.
- Trade farm and sustained pressure against helping a fight. After an advantage, use your attacks on the next legal structure.
- An immobile barrage carry needs different protection and timing from a mobile poke carry or sanctuary carry.

## Support

- Protect and enable living allies, interrupt meaningful threats and help turn a won fight into progress.
- Compare shields, healing, control and information with spending resources on damage; missing health and incoming pressure matter.
- A catch support, mobile engager, lifeguard and ranged enchanter are different jobs. Supports may still farm or finish enemies when useful.

## Learning rewards and evidence

- The actual Nexus win/loss remains the terminal objective. Internal learning rewards update model weights; they do not mint club coins, prizes or card XP.
- A small bounded avatar-specific potential uses effective enemy champion health damage, allied damage following confirmed control, actual objective damage and living teammate health. Each specialization weights these differently.
- Training credits confirmed stun/root/knockup follow-up only while the recorded effect remains active and only for allied health damage, excluding the controller's own hit. This is a limited attribution measure, not proof the control caused victory.
- A cast, missed hook, unused shield, raw damage taken, walking distance or repeated animation has no separate success bonus. Healing/shielding is represented by team survival; exact per-spell rescue attribution is not yet measured.
- Reward shaping is a discounted potential difference, with zero potential at real termination. Loops cannot create undiscounted progress bonuses. Censored matches bootstrap; they never receive a fabricated win.
- Learning rewards and instruction imitation loss are training signals. Independent matched complete games determine whether a checkpoint improved competitive results.
